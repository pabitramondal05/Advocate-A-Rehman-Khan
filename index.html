Q: Now What legal action take against me for corporate fraud?

"I committed a fraud while working in a private limited company. The total value of the product was 19,68,540. Out of which I paid 10 lakh, I could not pay the rest... Now can that company take legal steps against me?"

Advocate Rehman Khan answered: Yes. The company can take criminal and civil action against you. Under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), this amounts to a criminal offence. Under Section 316 BNS (Criminal Breach of Trust) and Section 318 BNS (Cheating). The company can file an FIR against you. Your best option is to try to settle the matter with the company and pay or agree on a payment plan before a criminal case is filed.

Q: My wife filed divorce case but still not received any summons

"My wife filed a divorce case in Siliguri court and I have not received any summons yet. I am presently living in Kathmandu... I don't know if she has filed any complaints or FIR in police station."

Advocate Rehman Khan answered: 1. Since you haven't received summons officially through court procedure/diplomatic channels, your appearance is not mandatory, but it's wise to keep track to avoid ex-parte proceedings.
2. She is under no legal obligation to provide petition copies via WhatsApp.
3. I suggest checking with a lawyer locally or via RTI to the police station to ensure no 498A IPC or Domestic Violence Act FIRs are filed, which could result in coercive action.

Q: Property disputes and forced signatures

"Mere papa se black magic/dhamki ki help se dushman ne sign karwa liya hai 50% property ke paper par... abh dhamki deta hai ki baki mummy wala bhi hathiya lega."

Advocate Rehman Khan answered: Indian law only recognizes evidence-based facts. Black magic is not a valid reason unless there is proof of coercion, fraud, or undue influence. However, if signatures were obtained by force/threat, you can file an FIR under IPC Sections 415-420 (Cheating), 503 (Criminal Intimidation), and file a civil suit for cancellation of the document under Section 31 of the Specific Relief Act. Since the mother's 50% share was not signed, it remains safe.
